Anthropogenic climate change has lengthened growing season of most temperate trees, which should generally increase growth \citep{gaoEarlierStartThermal2022}. Recent evidence, however, has showed that trees may not grow more with warmer seasons \citep{dow_warm_2022,silvestro_longer_2023,matula_shifts_2023}, highlighting gaps in our basic model of how season length affects tree growth. Alongside this, other new work suggests earlier seasons may not always be longer growing seasons \citep{zohner_effect_2023}. Here we use a rare combination of full growing season length data via vegetative phenology, and growth via tree rings to address the question how both the start, end, and thermal conditions during the growing season affects growth. % The favourable context of our common garden allows us to answer this fundamental question under drought- and competition-limited conditions. Our paper adds important information to the question that will help us understand whether forests will regenerate and store carbon with future climate.

\textit{What hypotheses or questions does this work address?}  
Do juvenile trees grow more with longer and warmer seasons? We address this question in a common garden where drought- and competition are limited. We test how longer warmer seasons affect growth through shifts in the start and end of season, and determine whether provenance latitude affects our conclusions. 

\textit{How does this work advance our current understanding of plant science?}  
By a using multi-year, multi-species high quality phenology and growth dataset, we are able to tease apart the contribution of start and end of season events alongside longer versus warmer seasons. Surprisingly, we show that end of season events---most often overlooked---appeared to be more important for growth than spring events by lengthening the thermal season.

\textit{Why is this work important and timely?}
Our work brings new insights on how temperature and end of season events may determine whether trees grow more with climate change \citep{dow_warm_2022,silvestro_longer_2023,matula_shifts_2023}. 
We show that the end of season appears more related to growth than the start of season, extending new work suggesting both early and late season events together dynamically determine growth \citep{zohner_effect_2023}. This suggests research focusing solely on the start of season could miss when longer seasons lead to more growth \citep{dow_warm_2022}. %emwOct25 -- could cut last sentence if needed.
